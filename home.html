Why this matters

The problem isn’t reading the report. It’s that no one asks the question.

Valley fever (coccidioidomycosis) comes from breathing fungal spores kicked up by dust storms and dry-season winds — something Arizona has no shortage of. On a chest X-ray or CT it looks like ordinary pneumonia, so it’s often treated as bacterial. The antibiotics can’t work on a fungus, and the diagnosis slips.

What usually happens

Read as pneumonia

Cough, fatigue, night sweats and a nodule or infiltrate on imaging → a course of antibiotics that a fungal infection ignores. The patient comes back weeks later, no better.

What should happen

One question, one test

“Could this be valley fever?” prompts a Coccidioides serology. If a rare bone, skin, or brain complication is brewing, catching it early changes everything.

How it works

Three steps, about a minute, nothing saved.

Paste your report

Drop in the text of your chest X-ray or CT report — or skip it entirely. MedScan highlights the findings that matter, right against your own words.

Answer six questions

How long you’ve coughed, whether antibiotics helped, where you’ve been. Fast radio buttons — no typing, no account.

Get a page for your doctor

A clear risk band with the reasoning shown line by line, plus a one-page test request you can print and hand to your clinician.

Model in training — integrating soon

A CT-scan model, trained on real chest imaging.

We’re training a computer-vision model to read chest CT scans for pneumonia-pattern findings — the cavities, nodules, and infiltrates that valley fever hides behind. When it’s validated, it will read the image directly instead of relying on the text report alone.

Until then, MedScan stays honest about its limits: the image alone can’t separate fungal from bacterial pneumonia — history, time course, and a blood test do. So today the tool works from your report text and answers, and flags when the question was never asked.
